05 / Mechanism

The chain already dates every shielded spend.

01 The tree

A fingerprint per block

Every block ends with a root: one fingerprint of every shielded coin that exists at that moment.

02 The anchor

Every spend names one

A spend must point at one of those roots and prove its coins are inside it. Consensus rejects it otherwise.

03 The check
Mainnet

Older root, older coins

If the named root is older than the event, the coins were already there. Nothing else about them is revealed.

No blocklist. No new cryptography. A rule Zcash already enforces on every spend.

07 / Limits

What it proves, and what it doesn't.

Proves
  • Every Ironwood coin the deposit spends was already in the pool before the event block.
  • It holds by consensus. Nobody can fake an older anchor.
  • It covers every event after the anchor at once.
Doesn't
  • Date coins received after the event. They need the payer-side path.
  • Cover transparent, Sapling or Orchard inputs.
  • Say who sent the coins or how much. It only rules out known events.
  • Hide that you chose to prove it. An old anchor is visible on chain, so use it only when you deposit somewhere that already knows you.
  • Make it mandatory. It's yours to offer, never a rail's to demand from everyone.

09 / Questions

Questions, answered.

What does Vintage prove?

That every shielded coin a transaction spends was already in Zcash's Ironwood pool before a block you choose. It says nothing about who sent the coins or how many.

Why would anyone need that?

After a hack, screening can't tell stolen shielded coins from anyone else's, and exit rails can hold shielded deposits for review. A Vintage proof shows your coins were there before the hack, so they can't be the stolen ones.

Can a proof be faked?

No. Zcash consensus checks every spend's anchor. Coins that arrived after the event aren't in any earlier state of the pool, so no valid transaction can spend them under an earlier anchor.

What do I give up?

No keys, amounts or history. The one visible trace: your transaction uses an older anchor than most, so use it where you're already known, like an exchange account.

What if I received my coins after the event?

Ask the person paying you to send a Vintage payment. You can then link your deposit to it with one single-use account's viewing key. A zero-knowledge version that shows no key exists as a research prototype.

Is there a blocklist?

No. The registry only labels block heights, like the Bitget hack's. Nothing marks a coin or a person as bad, and a failed check is not a flag.

Do I have to trust you?

No. The verifier is open source and reads public chain data. Run vintage verify against your own node and you get the same answer. Our public verifier keeps no logs.
