Events

Pick a date. Prove your coins are older.

An event is a block height that matters: where a hack's coins first arrived, a tax cutoff, the start of a bug. A Vintage proof says one thing about it: every coin in this transaction was already in the shielded pool before that block.

01 / How an event works

Three steps, one block height.

01 Something happens

An event gets a block height H: the first block where it matters. The Bitget hack's coins first entered the shielded pool at block 3,501,240.

02 Your coins were there

You send with an anchor from before H. Coins that arrived after H aren't in that snapshot, so your wallet can't include them, and consensus checks it.

03 Anyone checks

The txid and H are all a checker needs: our verifier, the API, or vintage verify on their own machine.

02 / Timeline

Ironwood so far.

Not to scale. Coins untouched since before an event clear it, and every later event, with one proof.

03 / Live registry

The signed list of dates.

The registry labels block heights, with the transactions and public sources behind each. It names no people and no addresses, and nothing in it marks a coin as bad. You can always check against a height of your own.

04 / Event types

One proof, any dated event.

HackLive

Exploit inflows

When

You prove

Who asks

Tax cutoffDraft law

Bought before a date

When
A legal cutoff. A German draft, not yet law, would keep the one-year exemption only for coins bought before 2027.
You prove
You've held these exact coins since before the cutoff: a coin can't change hands without being spent.
Who asks
You, your accountant, a platform.
Bug windowReady

Pre-bug coins

When
A bug that could create or move coins becomes possible.
You prove
Your coins are older than the window, so they weren't made through it.
Who asks
Exchanges, auditors.
HoldingFrom Jul 2027

Held a year

When
One year before you sell.
You prove
The exact coins you sell are over a year old. Ironwood launched in July 2026, so full-year proofs start in July 2027.
Who asks
Tax rules with a holding period.
